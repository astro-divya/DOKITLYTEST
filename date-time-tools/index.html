Choose whether you are counting elapsed time or calendar days

Calendar arithmetic needs a clear reference date and a definition of the interval. Calendar days, working days, weekdays and elapsed hours are not interchangeable, particularly around weekends and public holidays.

Choose the right starting point

The difference between two dates can mean elapsed days, inclusive calendar dates or working days. Decide which convention applies before selecting the calculator.

A practical example

For planning a project, a deadline may exclude weekends and local public holidays. Counting only weekdays is not always equivalent to counting the organization’s working days.

What to check before sharing the result

When adding months, end-of-month dates deserve attention because different months have different lengths. Time-zone changes can also affect exact hour totals.

Accuracy, quality and limitations

Check the start and end dates, whether endpoints are included and whether the result should be a count of days, weeks or business periods.

Frequently asked date time questions

Why does the date picker look different?

On mobile, native date pickers may display dates in a different order from the page labels. Verify month and day fields explicitly, especially when the two numbers are both 12 or less.

What if my day count is off by one?

If the answer is off by one, check inclusive versus exclusive counting. For hour-level differences, verify daylight-saving behavior and timezone assumptions instead of treating a calendar date as a fixed 24-hour interval.

What happens when a deadline falls on a holiday?

The correct handling depends on the agreement, workplace and country. A weekday calculator may exclude Saturday and Sunday but does not automatically know every locally observed holiday. Confirm the rule used in your application before promising a deadline.
